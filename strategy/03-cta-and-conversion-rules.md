# CTA and conversion rules

## The rule that matters most

**One post, one ask.** A post that asks for a comment and a DM and a link tap gets
none of them. Pick the destination before you write the first line.

## The six destinations

| Destination | Use when | Friction | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Comment keyword** | You want reach. Comments feed distribution | Lowest | Reels, high reach posts, lead magnets |
| **DM / story reply** | The ask is personal or specific | Low | Stories, cultural posts, "tell us your ceremonies" |
| **Link in bio** | They need a real page: guide, article, form | Medium | Carousels, article features, lead magnets |
| **WhatsApp** | They are ready to talk to a human | Medium | Warm audiences, proof posts, recaps |
| **Enquiry form** | They are ready to give real details | Highest | Only for people who already trust you |
| **Nothing** | Aesthetic posts | None | 40 percent of the feed |

**"Nothing" is a real choice.** An aesthetic post with no CTA is doing its job.

## How to ask without cheapening the brand

The difference between luxury and salesy is not volume. It is **who the ask serves.**

A cheap ask serves the brand: "DM us to book!" "Limited spots!" "Don't miss out!"
A luxury ask serves the reader: it hands them something specific and says what
happens next.

**The formula that works:**

> [What they do] and [what you send] [how fast]. [What they do not have to do.]

> Comment VIP and we send you the real cost ranges for a Bali wedding at your guest
> count, the same day. No form, no call.

That works because it names the deliverable, the speed, and removes the thing they
are afraid of, which is being put into a sales process.

## Banned CTA patterns

These read as cheap and they will be rejected in review.

- Urgency that is not real. "Only 3 dates left for 2027." If it is not true, never.
- Countdown pressure. "Book before prices go up."
- Begging. "We would love to hear from you", "no pressure", "if you're interested".
- Vague. "DM for more info." More info about what.
- Emoji arrow spam. "DM us 👉👉👉"
- All caps shouting. "BOOK NOW"
- Anything with an exclamation mark in the ask.

## Soft sell versus hard sell

**Soft sell** is the default. The post is genuinely useful on its own, and the ask is
the natural next step for someone who found it useful.

> A tea ceremony needs a real indoor room, close to the action. A lawn is not a room.
> Tell us your ceremonies and we will tell you how many spaces you actually need.

The reader got the value whether or not they act.

**Hard sell** is allowed roughly one post in ten, and only on proof or offer pillars.
It names the offer directly. It still never uses false urgency.

> Send us your date. You get a real price range in your currency the same day, and a
> venue shortlist built from your events.

**Never hard sell on an aesthetic post.** A beautiful wedding photograph with a
booking pitch attached is the single fastest way to cheapen this brand.

## The handoff rule

The CTA must be the answer to the post. If a carousel teaches that you should count
your events before choosing a venue, the ask is "tell us your ceremonies", not "send
us your date". A mismatched ask reads as a template, and people feel it instantly.

## Caption structure

**Put the ask in the first line.** Most readers never tap "more".

```
[The ask, in one line with the deliverable and the timing]

[The hook line from slide 1]

[The body, one short paragraph per idea]

[The ask again, in two or three words]
```

## Before any post ships

The destination must exist and must be tested. A comment keyword with no automation
behind it, or a link in bio pointing at a page that does not load, wastes the entire
post. See `qa/pre-publish-checklist.md`.
