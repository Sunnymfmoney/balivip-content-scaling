# Voice

Longer notes behind the rules in `CLAUDE.md`. Read this once, then work from
`CLAUDE.md`.

---

## Who is reading

Couples planning a wedding in Bali from somewhere else. Australia, Singapore, India,
Hong Kong, the UK. They already know they want a Bali wedding and they already know
planners exist. They have not picked one.

So do not sell them the idea of Bali. They are sold. Sell them the reason to stop
looking.

## What they are actually buying

Read the reviews in `reference/reviews.md` in order and the pattern is obvious.

About a third of them open by naming fear. They are wiring a large sum to strangers
in a country most of them have never visited, for a day that cannot be rescheduled,
based on a website and a WhatsApp thread. **That** is the purchase.

They are not shopping for a beautiful wedding. Everyone has beautiful photographs.
They are shopping for a way to stop being afraid.

Which means the strongest content is not aspirational. It is operational. It shows
that somebody has thought about the thing they have not thought about yet.

## The four things that break the fear

In the order they show up in the reviews.

1. **Fast, human replies.** Speed of response is named constantly.
2. **Somebody who has run this exact thing before.**
3. **Warmth in the first conversation.** Couples say outright this is why they chose.
4. **Willingness to bend.** The most commercially valuable thing in the whole review
   corpus. BaliVIP has beaten culture specialists who knew the customs better,
   because they were willing to build the wedding the couple actually imagined.

Point 4 is worth more than any award. It is the Naresh Ahuja review, and it is why
that review sits on the culture carousel even though it says a competitor knew the
customs better. It is the client's own words making the argument, which is stronger
than the brand making it.

## Tone

Confident and abundant. BaliVIP is the most capable option in Bali. Nothing should
read like it is asking permission or hoping for the booking.

That does not mean loud. The buyers describe the brand as calm. Calm and certain, not
calm and passive.

## Sentences

Short. Common words. Roughly third grade reading level.

Name the actual thing. Vague competence claims are invisible; specifics are proof.

> "We handle cultural requirements with care."

means nothing. This does:

> "A baraat needs a driveway and room to turn around. Some of Bali's best venues are
> reached single file down a cliff path. Ask how the groom arrives."

The second one proves the first one without claiming it.

## Where authority comes from now

Not from titles. The elected chairwoman and wedding association credential is removed
from everything, at the client's request. Do not reinstate it.

Use these instead:

- **The invisible fix.** What went wrong that the couple never found out about.
- **The questions you ask that others do not.** Can the kitchen run a separate prep
  line. Where does the groom's car turn around. Is the tea ceremony room a real room.
- **Bali specifics.** Permits, tides, traffic, rain, curfews.
- **Irreversibility.** You get one take at this day.

## Hooks

A hook has to stop a scroll. Kinetic footage, crowd energy, motion, celebration.

Never open on a static seated portrait, including the founder's. It is excellent
later in a video for credibility, and it is wrong as the first frame.

## CTAs

One ask per post. Name what they get and when they get it.

> Comment VIP and we send you the real cost ranges for a Bali wedding at your guest
> count, the same day. No form, no call.

That works because it says what arrives, how fast, and what they do not have to do.

Imperative, never hedged. "WhatsApp us now" beats "Message us your date."

## Captions

Put the ask in the first line. Most readers never tap "more". Then earn it
underneath, and repeat the ask at the end.

## Things that have already failed

Learn from these rather than rediscovering them.

- **Trade jargon.** "Floor and ceiling" and "no discovery call before the discovery
  call" both shipped. The internal team had to ask what they meant. If your own team
  has to ask, it does not publish.
- **Counts.** Venue and vendor counts were wrong every single time they went out. A
  count sets an expectation somebody then has to deliver.
- **A trimmed review.** A review was cut with ellipses to make it fit a card, and the
  cut removed the names of the people being praised. Never trim a review.
- **Claims with no source.** If nobody can say where a number came from, it is not a
  fact, it is a liability.
