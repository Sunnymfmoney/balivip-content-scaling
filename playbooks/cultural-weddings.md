# Playbook: Cultural and destination weddings

**Goal** Prove the thing competitors cannot fake. This pillar wins the enquiries that
are worth the most.

**When to use** Weekly. It is also the entire job of pinned post 2.

## Why this matters more than it looks

BaliVIP has beaten planners who knew the customs better, because they were willing to
build the wedding the couple actually imagined. That is the Naresh Ahuja review and it
is the most commercially valuable sentence the brand owns.

So the argument is **not** "we are the biggest experts in your culture". It is: **we
ask what your family actually does, and we build for that.** Expertise plus
flexibility beats expertise alone.

## The rule that makes it credible

**Name the actual thing.** Rituals by name, and what each one physically requires.
Vague respect reads as ignorance.

| Ritual | What it physically needs |
|---|---|
| Baraat | A driveway, a turning circle, room for a horse or a car and a dhol player |
| Haldi / Mehendi | Daytime space, seating, mess tolerance, a change of clothes |
| Sangeet | Loud, late, a stage, a dance floor, a curfew that allows it |
| Tea pai | A real indoor room, close to the action. A lawn is not a room |
| Sangjit | Often the day before. Some couples skip it entirely |
| Akad | Quiet, seated, often needs a specific orientation |
| Ceremony and reception | Two spaces, one evening, built to feel intimate |

## Rules

- **"International", never "Western".** Most BaliVIP weddings are mixed.
- **Never state a fixed number of events for a culture.** It varies by family. A
  Chinese wedding might run four things or two.
- **Parents are frequently the decision makers.** Plan for them, and say so.
- **Food is not a detail.** Jain, halal, vegetarian, no beef, allergies. Ask whether
  the kitchen can run a separate prep line.

## Hook templates

- What your [culture] wedding actually needs in Bali.
- A [ritual] needs more than [assumption].
- [Ritual A] is quiet and close. [Ritual B] is loud and late.
- Your parents will have a lot to say about this. Good.
- We do not plan around your family. We plan with them.

## CTA options

Tell us your ceremonies (best) · comment keyword · link in bio.

"Tell us your ceremonies" outperforms everything else here, because it is the exact
thing the reader is worried nobody will ask.

## Common mistakes

- Generic "we celebrate all cultures" with no specifics. Worse than saying nothing.
- Stating an event count for a culture.
- Using "Western".
- Treating a culture as decor rather than as logistics.
- One photograph of one Indian wedding standing in for all of South Asia.
