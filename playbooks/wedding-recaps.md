# Playbook: Wedding recaps

**Goal** Aesthetic reach and proof at the same time. The most shareable thing the
brand makes.

**When to use** Every wedding worth showing, once the couple has agreed.

## Consent first

**Never post a couple before they have agreed.** Get it in writing, and get it clear:
feed, stories, ads, or all three. A couple who sees their wedding used in an ad they
did not agree to is a problem no post is worth.

## The shapes

**The 30 second recap.** Arrival, ceremony moment, the room revealed, the party. Ends
on the couple. No teaching, no ask.

**The "what it took" post.** The finished room, then what stood behind it: setup
timelapse, the team, the numbers you can actually say. This is the format that makes
people understand what they are paying for.

**The diary.** Three to five stories across the day. Highest engagement of anything.

**The one detail.** A single thing done properly: the mini fans, the slippers, the
separate prep line. Small, specific, memorable.

## Structure for the "what it took" version

1. The finished thing. Beautiful.
2. Rewind. What it looked like six hours earlier.
3. One number you can actually source.
4. One thing that went wrong and was fixed.
5. The couple's own words, if there is a review.

## Hook templates

- [N] hours to build. [N] hours to enjoy.
- This is what it looked like six hours earlier.
- [Culture] wedding, [N] events, [N] days.
- The bit nobody sees.
- She said [one line from the review].

## CTA options

None for pure recap · link in bio to reviews · WhatsApp for warm audiences.

**Most recaps should have no ask.** They earn the right to ask elsewhere.

## Common mistakes

- Posting without consent.
- Only showing the pretty part. The setup is the proof.
- Naming the venue without clearance.
- Inventing numbers. "500 guests" because it looked like a lot.
- Turning a beautiful recap into a sales pitch in the caption.

## Examples

1. Setup timelapse into the finished room, one cut, no words.
2. The baraat, full energy, 15 seconds, no ask.
3. "More staff than guests" with the Denis Vodišek review over setup footage.
4. The mini fans and the slippers, from the Piyumi review. One detail, one frame.
5. A three day Indian wedding as a story diary, one highlight per event.
