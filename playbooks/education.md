# Playbook: Planning education

**Goal** Enquiries. This pillar produces more than any other, because it proves
competence and creates the "I had not thought of that" moment.

**When to use** At least twice a week across formats.

## The engine

Find something the couple has not thought about, that has a physical consequence,
that you handle. That is the post.

Good education content is **specific and operational**, never general advice.

> "Plan early and communicate with your vendors"

is worthless. This is not:

> "A baraat needs a driveway and room to turn around. Some of Bali's best venues are
> reached single file down a cliff path. Ask how the groom arrives."

## Where the ideas come from

1. **Planning calls.** Every question a couple asks twice is a post. See
   `sop/calls-to-faq-content.md`.
2. **The reviews.** Every relieved sentence names a fear worth teaching to.
3. **Things that went wrong** and were fixed. The save is the story.
4. **The questions your planners ask venues.** Each one is a post nobody else can write.

## Structure

**The "do not" shape**, which is the highest performing:

1. Name the mistake as an imperative. "Do not choose the venue first."
2. Say why in one line. "The room is the easy part."
3. Say what to do instead. "Count your events first."

**The "what it actually needs" shape:**

1. Name the thing. "A tea ceremony."
2. Name the physical requirement. "Needs a real indoor room, close to the action."
3. Name the failure. "A lawn is not a room."

## Hook templates

- Do not [mistake].
- [Thing] needs more [space / time / people] than you think.
- Nobody tells you this about [thing].
- Ask your planner this before you book.
- [Two things] are not the same thing.
- If [condition], walk away.
- The question that saves you [outcome].

## CTA options

Comment keyword · tell us your [X] · link in bio to the guide.

The ask must answer the lesson. Teach about events, ask about ceremonies.

## Common mistakes

- Generic advice available on any blog.
- Teaching without ever asking for anything. Education is 40 percent of the feed and
  it should still convert.
- A lesson that implies BaliVIP made the mistake.
- Naming a venue as the bad example. Never.

## Examples

1. Do not choose the venue first. Count your events.
2. Do not assume a destination wedding is cheap. You are funding a whole celebration.
3. A tea ceremony is quiet and close. A sangeet is loud and late. Same lighting for
   both and each one feels slightly wrong.
4. Ask whether the kitchen can run a separate prep line.
5. Do not pick a planner on experience alone. The most weddings behind them is not
   the same as the most attention on yours.
