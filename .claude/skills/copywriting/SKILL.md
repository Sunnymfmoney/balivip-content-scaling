# Copy that sells without sounding like it

Six ideas do almost all the work. Everything else is execution.
Detail and worked examples in `reference/frameworks.md`. Layout and review rules below.

## 1. They are the hero. You are the guide.

The reader is Luke. You are Yoda. Yoda has scars, a plan and a stake, and about nine lines.

Practical test: **count the sentences that open with your name, "we", or "our".** If more
than a third of them do, the copy is about you. Rewrite so each one lands on something the
reader gets, not something you do.

- "We shortlist venues against your events." → what they get: a shortlist that fits.
- Better: "Your shortlist is built from your events, not from photographs."

"We" is not banned. A guide who never speaks has no authority. But every "we" line has to
end on the reader's side of the table.

## 2. You cannot create desire. You can only channel it.

Nobody wants your product. They arrive already wanting something. Your job is to attach the
thing they already want to the thing you sell.

Ask: **what did they want before they ever heard of us?** Write to that, then bridge.

## 3. Match the awareness stage, or you will be ignored

| Where they are | What the first line must do |
|---|---|
| Doesn't know they have the problem | Name the problem in their words |
| Knows the problem, not the solutions | Name the outcome |
| Knows the solutions, not you | Name the mechanism, what is different |
| Knows you, not convinced | Name the proof |
| Ready | Name the offer and the ask |

Most copy fails by writing stage four at a stage one reader. A cold audience gets the
problem, never the mechanism.

## 4. Value is a ratio, so pull all four levers

**Dream outcome × likelihood it works, divided by time it takes × effort it costs.**

Most writers only push the numerator, harder and louder. The fastest gains are almost always
in the denominator: make it faster, make it easier, remove a step, remove a form, remove a
call. "Same day" and "no form" beat another adjective every time.

## 5. Every unit is hook, then story, then one ask

A carousel, a script, a page and an email are the same shape at different lengths. If you
cannot say the hook in one line, the piece has no spine yet.

## 6. Nothing ships that only works in sequence

Every slide, section and paragraph must survive being screenshotted alone and sent to one
person. If it only sets up the next one, it is not a unit, it is a transition. Cut it or
promote the fact inside it.

# Making a set feel like one conversation

Two units in a row are congruent when the second is the natural next question. Read the set
aloud and listen for a jolt.

**The handoff test.** After each unit, ask what a reader now wants to know. The next unit
answers exactly that. If it answers something else, the order is wrong, not the copy.

**Group before you polish.** Sort units into three or four blocks with a logic a reader can
feel: what to decide, what it costs, who to trust, what happens next. Two units on the same
idea sitting six apart reads as repetition. Adjacent, it reads as depth.

**One voice per set.** Pick the person and stay in it. Do not open in "you" and drift to
"a couple" and end in "clients".

**No orphan references.** Every pronoun, count and "these" needs its antecedent in the same
unit.

# CTAs

**One ask.** Not two verbs, not "or". Two asks halve both.

**Name what they get, and when.** "Comment VIP" is a mechanic, not an offer. "Comment VIP
and we send the real cost ranges at your guest count, same day" is an offer.

**Only promise what exists today.** A CTA that promises a guide, a dashboard or a document
nobody has built is the most expensive line in the piece. Check before you write it.

**Remove the next step, not just the friction.** "No form. No call first." is often worth
more than the entire body above it.

**Lead them there.** The unit before the CTA should already have them nodding. A CTA that
arrives cold has to sell from zero.

**Different asks across a set.** If three pieces run together, three different asks mapped
to funnel position: comment at the top, specifics in the middle, booking at the bottom.

**The caption rule.** The CTA goes in the **first line** of the caption, on every post and
every video. Most readers never expand. Say the ask, then earn it underneath.

# Reviews and testimonials

**Stars must look real.** Tight tracking, no gaps. Five glyphs with wide letter-spacing or
literal spaces between them reads as decoration, and decoration reads as fake. Set them
solid, sized to the name beside them, in one accent colour.

**A screenshot of the real review beats typeset text** whenever the screenshot is legible.
It carries the platform, the avatar and the date, which are the trust signals.

**Trim long reviews, by removal only.** Nobody reads 300 characters on a card. Keep the part
that builds trust and shows how they talk about you. Mark cuts with an ellipsis. **Never
reword a real person's sentence** to make it shorter, and never stitch two sentences into
one they did not write.

**Choose the quote for the claim it is next to.** A review about cultural flexibility belongs
beside the cultural piece, not beside the pricing piece.

**Pair the review with that reviewer's own photograph** where you have it. A review over a
stranger's photo is decoration; over their own wedding, their own kitchen, their own store,
it is evidence.

**Consent is a separate question from public.** Public on a review site is not the same as
asked. Raise it before it pins.

# The taste floor

Simple, clear, elegant. It holds for every brand.

- **Alignment.** Everything lines up to a grid. One left edge per block. If two elements are
  nearly aligned, they are misaligned.
- **Margins are equal and generous,** and they never change between units in a set. Uneven
  padding is the fastest way to look cheap.
- **One idea per unit.** If it needs two, it is two units.
- **Line breaks are authored, not left to chance.** No single word hanging alone on the last
  line. Measure against the real font at the real size, do not count characters.
- **Type does not sit on the subject.** Copy belongs in the empty part of the frame. Score
  placement on detail, not on darkness, or you will land on faces.
- **Restraint.** One accent colour, one type pairing, generous space. Anything that is only
  there to fill a gap comes out.
- **Every image earns its place.** No photograph appears twice in a set, and no two units
  lean on the same shoot. Match the picture to that unit's claim.
- **Pick the strongest assets you have.** In a photo-led set the work is curation before it
  is layout. Rank the library first, then write to the best of it.
